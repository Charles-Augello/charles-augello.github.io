Establishing an internal UX practice and design system

The organization relied on an outsourced design model, and product teams kept rebuilding the same UI patterns. I was tasked with establishing an in-house UX practice, and an accessible design system became its foundation.

Sole in-house designer; established the UX practice and led the design system

The decision to enable

Design had been outsourced, and product and engineering teams were repeatedly reinventing UI patterns, slowing delivery and increasing usability and compliance risk. Leadership needed to build an in-house capability that could move faster without compromising accessibility standards.

Key decision: Can an in-house UX practice, anchored by a standardized, accessible design system, materially increase delivery speed and consistency across teams?

Rapid insight and opportunity identification

As the practice’s only designer, I partnered with product, engineering, and marketing stakeholders to assess existing UI fragmentation. Through heuristic reviews, pattern inventories, and analysis of delivery bottlenecks, we identified the high-impact components that teams repeatedly rebuilt.

Within the first week, we prioritized a focused set of foundational components that would deliver the greatest speed and risk-reduction benefits.

System design and prototyping

In close collaboration with engineers, I co-created reusable UI components designed for both design and development efficiency:

  • Componentized layouts and controls
  • Variants supporting multiple states and contexts
  • Built-in accessibility considerations (contrast, focus, keyboard behavior)

Prototypes were validated with product teams and engineers to confirm usability, feasibility, and adoption readiness.

Accessibility standards, enforced by default

Accessibility was a non-negotiable requirement. The components themselves enforced accessibility standards: each one was designed to meet WCAG 2.1 AA and reviewed against it, so teams inherited compliance instead of re-checking it. Clear usage guidance helped teams apply components correctly without additional review cycles.

By embedding accessibility into the system, teams could move faster while reducing compliance risk.

Building the practice, not just the system

Replacing an outsourced model meant owning the discipline in-house. Alongside the system, I:

  • Introduced human-centered design and CX measurement practices
  • Partnered with agencies and internal teams to define brand-aligned, customer-centric journeys
  • Set standards and governance so the practice could scale beyond one person

Enablement and adoption

To ensure adoption, I focused on enablement as much as design:

  • Created clear documentation and usage guidelines
  • Partnered with engineers on implementation standards
  • Facilitated walkthroughs and working sessions with product teams
  • Coached engineers and product partners on using the system

This approach reduced friction and encouraged consistent use across teams.

Outcomes and impact

  • Established an internal UX practice, replacing an outsourced design model
  • Reduced design and development rework by ~30%
  • Accelerated prototyping and delivery cycles across multiple teams
  • Improved consistency and usability across customer-facing platforms
  • Reduced review effort by standardizing accessible patterns
  • Established a foundation for future system expansion

Scale readiness

The system was intentionally designed to scale:

  • Modular components extendable by future teams
  • Clear governance and contribution guidelines
  • Documentation supporting onboarding and reuse

These practices allowed new teams to move quickly without sacrificing quality.

Reflection: AI-first evolution

Today, I would further enhance the system by:

  • Using AI to detect pattern drift and inconsistencies
  • Accelerating component creation through AI-assisted variants
  • Generating real-time usage insights to guide system evolution

The principle remains unchanged: systems exist to make speed safe and repeatable.
